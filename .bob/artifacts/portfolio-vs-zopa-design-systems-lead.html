Key gaps to address

🔴 No native mobile evidence
Zopa's design system was rebuilt for native. The role requires someone "comfortable working across native mobile experiences." Your entire portfolio is enterprise web/desktop. This is a legitimate gap that could be a blocker at shortlist stage.
Action: If you have any native mobile design work — even from side projects or earlier in your career — find a way to surface it. If not, acknowledge it directly in your cover letter and frame your transferable skill: systems thinking and platform adoption translates across surfaces. Being honest and direct about it is better than hoping they don't notice.
🟡 Team management is implied, not shown
The Figma kit case study mentions "a team of 10" but spends almost no time on the people-management story. The "what surprised me most was the team" line is there — but it's one sentence. Zopa explicitly wants someone who manages and develops designers. This is a hard requirement.
Action: Add a "The team" or "How I led" section to the Figma kit case study that covers: how you structured the team, how you handled blockers, how you developed or supported people who struggled, and what you'd do differently. Even one more paragraph transforms this from "project lead" to "people manager."
🟡 AI in design systems workflows is not a headline story
The AI patent is buried in the Recognition tab. Zopa specifically called out Figma MCP and Claude as desirable experience, and the role involves "shaping AI-enabled workflows" as a top-line responsibility. Your forward-thinking instincts on AI are real — the patent proves it — but the framing doesn't land with urgency.
Action: Move the AI patent case study to the main Work tab. Consider a short dedicated section (even a paragraph) in your homepage hero or in a case study about how you currently use AI in your systems workflow — if you use Figma MCP, Claude for documentation, or anything similar, name it explicitly.
🟡 Senior stakeholder influence needs a concrete moment
Zopa wants evidence of "influencing senior stakeholders" and "setting trade-offs." Your work clearly involved this — getting 8 acquired product teams on board with a migration they didn't choose is a senior influencing story — but it's told as a team achievement rather than your specific influence over a named decision.
Action: In the migration case study, add one concrete moment: a decision that was contested, a stakeholder who pushed back, and how you navigated it. Even two sentences transforms the narrative from "we aligned" to "I persuaded."
🔵 System health metrics language is absent
The JD mentions "define and track measures of system health, adoption, consistency and impact." You have adoption numbers (165%, 21/21) but no language about health metrics, coverage scores, or operating model for measuring a system over time.
Action: In the Carbon migration or contributions case study, add a brief note about what system health looked like — even the compliance rubric you designed IS a system health measure. Frame it as such explicitly. Something like "the rubric gave us a consistent definition of readiness — our first measure of system health across eight products."
